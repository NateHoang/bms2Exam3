    • Clinical Forms of Candidiasis

    Form of Candidiasis Clinical Presentation Key Diagnostic / Clinical Feature
    Pseudomembranous
    • "Curdled milk" or "cottage cheese" white plaques.
    • Can be scraped off with a tongue blade.
    Acute Erythematous
    • Painful, red, denuded patches on the mucosa.
    • Often occurs following broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy such as steroid inhaler.
    • Antibiotics kill competing bacteria, allowing Candida overgrowth.
    Chronic Hyperplastic
    • Persistent bilateral white patches or plaques on buccal commissure regions
    • Observed in smokers and high alcohol consumers.
    • Cannot be scraped off.
    • Carries a higher risk of transforming into oral cancer.
    Chronic Erythematous (Denture Stomatitis)
    • Redness and inflammation limited to the area under a denture.
    • Most common on the palate.
    • Caused by ill-fitting dentures, or inadequate oral hygiene
    • No invasion of underlying epithelium.
    Angular Cheilitis
    • Redness, cracking, and crusting at the corners of the mouth.
    • Often a co-infection of Candida and bacteria such as Staph aureus or Streptococcus.
    Median Rhomboid Glossitis
    • Flat or raised red area of the filiform papillae with a red area in the central dorsal tongue.
    • Highly associated with smoking.
    • Highly associated with the use of inhaled steroids.
    • Kissing Lesion may develop on on hard palate
    Chronic Mucocutaneous
    • Severe, persistent infections of the skin, nails, and mucosa.
    • Impaired cellular immunity against Candida.
    • Endocrine disorders

    Important Virulence Factors of C. albicans

    • Adhesion: Uses adhesins to stick to host cells and dental materials, such as dentures.
    • Phenotypic Switching: The ability to switch between yeast for spread and hyphae for invading tissue.
    • Enzymes: Produces aspartyl proteinases and phospholipases that digest host cell membranes.

    Differential Diagnosis Tip

    If a white lesion in the mouth cannot be wiped away with gauze, consider Chronic Hyperplastic Candidiasis or Leukoplakia

    Section 3: Antigens vs. PAMPs

    Feature PAMPs Antigens
    Specificity Shared across pathogens (e.g., teichoic acid found in many bacteria) Specific to individual pathogens — need specific antibodies for each
    Immune System Triggered Innate immune system Adaptive immune system
    Definition Microbial molecules not found on host cells Any substance that elicits a specific immune response
    Key Feature — Each antigen has an epitope (antigenic determinant), the immunologically active region
    Types —
    • Exogenous antigens (on invading pathogens)
    • Endogenous antigens (within host cytosol — viral proteins, intracellular bacteria, tumor antigens)
    • Autoantigens (host proteins inducing self-immune response)

    Section 6: Macrophage Activation Stages

    Stage Triggers Characteristics
    Resting — Collects dead cell debris
    Primed / Activated
    • Pathogen recognition
    • IFN-γ signals
    • Engulfs invaders
    • Upregulates Class II MHC on cell surface
    • Functions as an antigen-presenting cell
    Hyperactivated Strong activation signals
    • Increased lysosomes
    • Increased ROS and nitrogen species
    • Can dump lysosomal contents onto multicellular parasites
    • Releases TNF to recruit neutrophils

    Section 7: Phagocytosis Process

    Step Description
    1. Attachment Bacterium becomes attached to membrane evaginations called pseudopodia
    2. Ingestion Bacterium is ingested, forming a phagosome
    3. Fusion Phagosome fuses with lysosome → phagolysosome
    4. Killing & Digestion
    • Bacterium killed by antimicrobial agents (defensins)
    • Low pH
    • Hydrolytic enzymes (proteases)
    • Oxidative burst (ROS, nitric oxide)
    5. Release Digestion products released from cell
    Activation: Direct Binding PAMPs bind directly to PRRs on phagocytic cells → also activates cytokine production
    Activation: Indirect (Opsonization) Opsonins (IgG, C3b) coat microbe → recognized by opsonin receptors on phagocytes → activates phagocytosis

    Section 8: Complement System

    Feature Details
    Composition ~30 serum proteins (serine proteases), synthesized by the liver
    Functions
    • Induce inflammation
    • Activate phagocytosis (opsonization)
    • Lyse susceptible microbes
    • Strengthen humoral immunity
    Activation Pathway Trigger Key Step
    Classical Pathway
    • Antigen-antibody interaction (secondary exposure)
    • C1q binds to antibodies (IgM, IgG) on microbe surface
    		 Proteolytic cascade → C4b2a = C3 convertase
    Alternative Pathway
    • Direct microbe recognition (primary exposure)
    • C3 directly recognizes microbial PAMPs
    		 C3 convertase cleaves C3
    Lectin Binding Pathway
    • Microbial mannose residues recognized by mannose-binding lectin (plasma protein from liver)
    • MASP forms C3 convertase
    		 C3 convertase (C2 + C4) cleaves C3
    Component Function
    C3 Convertase Cleaves C3 → C3a + C3b
    C3a
    • Anaphylatoxin → stimulates inflammation
    • Induces mast cell histamine release
    • Chemotaxin
    C3b
    • Opsonin — coats microbe surface → enhances phagocytosis
    • Binds C3 convertase to form C5 convertase
    C5 Convertase Cleaves C5 → C5a + C5b
    C5a Anaphylatoxin & chemotaxin (like C3a)
    C5b Binds C6–C9 → initiates Membrane Attack Complex (MAC)
    MAC (C9) Forms pore in bacterial membrane → lysis (water/nutrient escape → cell death)

    Section 10: Inflammation — The Hallmark of Innate Immunity

    Phase Events
    Initiation
    • Resident tissue cells (macrophages, mast cells, dendritic cells) activated by PAMPs/DAMPs
    • Release inflammatory cytokines
    • Upregulate selectins/integrins (increased leukocyte adherence)
    • Histamine & bradykinin increase vascular permeability
    • IL-6 & TNF-α stimulate C-reactive protein from hepatocytes → activates complement
    Leukocyte Extravasation Transmigration of leukocytes through capillary walls into inflamed tissue
    Elimination
    • Recruited leukocytes (neutrophils first, then macrophages) phagocytose bacteria
    • Amplify inflammation via inflammatory mediators
    • Macrophages help wound healing
    Clearance
    • Clear invading pathogens + damaged cells
    • Tissue remodeling
    • Induction of adaptive immune responses if needed
