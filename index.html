Lecture 1: Introduction to Immunology

Granulocytes

Cell Type Phagocytic Type Nucleus Type Cytoplasm Staining Primary Functions / Roles
Neutrophils Phagocytes Multi-lobed nucleus - -
  • Dominant first responders
  • Phagocytose bacteria and secrete antimicrobial and tissue remodeling proteins.
  • Main cellular components of pus
  • Short lifespan of only 1-2 days
    		•
    Eosinophils Motile Phagocytic Cells Bi-lobed nucleus - Binds to acid dyes like eosin (Smears red)
  • Defense against multicellular parasites (worms) and fungal infections.
  • Contribute to allergic reactions and asthma
    		•
    Basophils Non-phagocytic Kidney-bean shaped nucleus - Bind basic dyes = blue color in stained blood smears Major contributor to allergy symptoms via histamine release.
    • ↑cell permability
    • Smooth muscle contraction
    Mast Cells Non-phagocytic Large central nucleus Cytoplasmic granules that contains histamine - Mature mast cells are adherent to small blood vessels in a wide variety of tissues
    • Skin
    • Mucosal epithelial tissue of the respiratory
    • Genitourinary tracts
    • Digestive tracts

    Myeloid Antigen Presenting Cells

    Cell Type Phagocytic Type Nucleus Type Cytoplasm Staining Primary Functions / Roles
    Monocytes Mononuclear Phagocytic Cells Kidney bean-shaped nucleus
  • Finely granular cytoplasm
  • Cytoskeletal filaments
    		•  - Differentiate into macrophages and dendritic cells
    Macrophages Mononuclear Phagocytes Mononuclear (Single nucleus) - - Tissue-resident macrophages
    • Bone: Osteoclasts
    • CNS & Brain: Microglial cells
    • Lungs: Alveolar macrophages
    • Liver: Kupffer cells
    • Skin/Oral Mucosa: Langerhans cells
    • Spleen: Red pulp macrophages to scavenge RBCs and recycle iron
    • Heart: Cardiac resident macrophages promote angiogenesis, remove ageing and dying cells and participate in cardiac electrical condution. Play a role in CVS and cardiac physiology
    Recruited or monocyte-derived
    • Migrate into tissues during a response to infection
    • M1 macrophages (Classically activated): inflammatory macrophages that ingest microbes and activate the adaptive immunity
    • M2 Macrophages (Alternatively activated): Macrophages have immunosuppressive and tissue remodeling phenotype
    Dendritic Cells Phagocytic Cells Irregularly shaped (with long cytoplasmic processes) - -
  • Act as a bridge between innate and adaptive responses
  • Capture and present antigens→present antigen to T-cells→Initiates adaptive immune response
